# Universal visual review criteria

Use these criteria for every artifact. Judge relationships and purpose, not isolated decoration.

## 1. Goal and communication

- Identify the one message/action the audience should retain.
- Check whether the first impression matches the intended category, tone, and audience.
- Check content accuracy, missing essentials, misleading emphasis, and whether decorative signals contradict the message.
- Distinguish communication failure from stylistic preference.

## 2. Hierarchy and reading path

- Verify a clear entry point, primary/secondary/tertiary levels, and a deliberate exit or CTA.
- Check whether size, position, contrast, weight, color, isolation, and detail all compete to signal importance. Too many simultaneous signals create false hierarchy.
- Inspect at thumbnail scale: core grouping and focal point should remain apparent even when details disappear.
- Ensure supporting copy does not visually overpower the title, subject, data, or action.

## 3. Composition, grid, and balance

- Find the governing grid, axes, margins, columns, modules, baseline relationships, or intentional anti-grid.
- Check repeated elements for common alignment and distribution.
- Compare geometric alignment with optical alignment; circles, triangles, serif forms, and irregular subjects often need optical compensation.
- Evaluate visual mass, negative space, tension, crop, edge relationships, and center of gravity.
- Report inconsistent exceptions, not every deviation.

## 4. Spacing and grouping

- Apply proximity: related elements should form clear units; unrelated groups need visibly larger separation.
- Look for a repeatable spacing rhythm rather than demanding one universal value.
- Check internal padding against external gaps, edge breathing room, accidental tangencies, near-misses, trapped whitespace, and inconsistent gutters.
- Treat whitespace as hierarchy and pacing, not merely unused area.

## 5. Typography

- Check type-role clarity: display, heading, subheading, body, metadata, label, number.
- Inspect family compatibility, weight, width, optical size, tracking, leading, line length, alignment, punctuation, CJK/Latin/numeral mixing, and awkward line breaks.
- Minimize unnecessary type voices while preserving deliberate contrast.
- Treat hierarchy signals as a system. Use spatial cues such as placement, indentation, line/paragraph spacing and graphic cues such as size, weight, style, and color consistently across equivalent content.
- Check whether redundant cues strengthen navigation or merely create noise. A critical distinction may use more than one cue, but every level does not need every cue.
- Inspect grids as programs for organizing content, not compulsory cages. A deliberate grid break should clarify emphasis, pacing, or meaning.
- Avoid arbitrary horizontal or vertical type distortion unless the distortion is an intentional, controlled display technique.
- Check character-shape collisions, widows/orphans where relevant, cramped caps, and weak word shapes.
- Judge legibility at final physical/digital size and viewing distance, not only on a zoomed canvas.
- Do not identify a font unless source metadata makes it certain.

## 6. Color and contrast

- Identify palette roles: background/surface, primary, accent, text, semantic, data categories.
- Check hue, value, chroma/saturation, temperature, area proportion, and whether colors share a coherent role system.
- Inspect whether high-chroma colors dominate too much area, whether multiple accents compete, and whether neutrals have unwanted hue drift.
- Verify that color is not the sole carrier of essential meaning; add text, shape, icon, pattern, or position where needed.
- For web/UI and essential digital content, target WCAG 2.2 AA: normal text 4.5:1, large text 3:1; relevant non-text UI/graphics commonly need 3:1. Treat screenshot estimates as risks, not measurements.

## 7. Image, illustration, icon, and shape system

- Check subject relevance, crop, resolution, masking, edge quality, perspective, light direction, shadow grammar, noise/grain, and color grading.
- For icon sets, compare optical size, visual weight, stroke width/caps/joins, corner radius, fill/outline mode, detail density, perspective, and bounding-box breathing room.
- For repeated shapes, check radius, border weight, shadow, depth, and construction complexity.
- Avoid forcing unlike silhouettes to identical physical size; normalize optical volume and center of gravity.

## 8. Consistency and intentionality

- Find the repeated grammar in type, color, spacing, corner, stroke, texture, imagery, and motion cues.
- Treat one-off choices as suspect when they lack semantic or compositional purpose.
- Preserve meaningful exceptions that create focus or brand character.
- For a series, evaluate invariant elements and controlled variation separately.

## 9. Accessibility and delivery

- Check essential legibility, contrast risk, color dependence, small labels, dense paragraphs, flashing/motion implications if visible, and text over variable imagery.
- Consider screen, print, projection, outdoor, social-feed, and thumbnail conditions.
- Check safe areas, bleed/crop risks, platform overlays, export resolution, file format, and alternate text/data-table needs when applicable.
- Do not claim print color, bleed, responsive behavior, or accessibility compliance from a single screenshot.

## Useful diagnostic tests

Use only when appropriate:

- 3-second test: what is seen and understood first?
- Thumbnail test: does hierarchy survive reduction?
- Grayscale/value test: does hierarchy rely only on hue?
- Blur/squint test: are major masses and focal contrast coherent?
- Edge test: are margins, crops, and tangencies intentional?
- Repetition audit: do equivalent components share a grammar?
- Content removal test: does every element earn its place?
- Distance test: can presentation/poster essentials be read in context?
